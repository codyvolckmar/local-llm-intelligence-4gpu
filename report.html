Local LLM Intelligence Report — 4 GPU Tiers

Hunting the best local-model intelligence (reasoning + coding/agentic + long-context document work) attainable on an RTX 3090, RTX 4090, RTX 5090, and RTX 6000 Ada (48 GB). Speeds are memory-bandwidth-scaled estimates from measured 3090 figures — decode is bandwidth-bound, so bandwidth ratio is the honest scaling law. Marked °.

What each card actually changes

These four cards sit in three distinct capability brackets, not four. The 4090 does not raise the intelligence ceiling vs the 3090 (same 24 GB) — it only speeds it up slightly. The real steps up are the 5090 at 32 GB and the RTX 6000 Ada at 48 GB, and they jump different axes: the 5090 is the speed / full-context / NVFP4 card; the 6000 Ada is the capacity card that finally unlocks bigger models and higher quant precision — the biggest raw-intelligence gain of the group.

6000 Ada note: at ~960 GB/s it decodes like a 3090-class card for same-size models, but its whole value is 2×-the-memory — bigger models, higher quant, longer context — and NVLink can pair two for 96 GB. Its ~0.70 $/hr running cost is the highest of the four, which matters when comparing $/token against cheap frontier APIs (see the frontier section). A 5090 NVFP4 stack can be ~3× faster (Blackwell W4A4), but only outside Ollama via vLLM/TensorRT-LLM.

The verdict that changes minds: electricity is no longer a slam-dunk saving. At 0.70 $/hr, a 6000 Ada burns ~$510/month running 24/7 and still costs more per token than the far smarter DeepSeek V4-Flash API. Local's real wins today are privacy, data residency, unlimited volume, no rate limits, no lock-in — not raw $/token. In late-2026 terms: local is a sovereignty play, and only a good cost play past roughly low-millions of output tokens/month where amortized electricity beats even DeepSeek's rate. The one thing no API gives you that the 48GB card does: the frontier-adjacent 80B brain in your own hands.

The 48GB tier — RTX 6000 Ada and the family around it

The 48GB "capacity" tier is the only real IQ jump. 48 GB fits the strongest model that runs on a single box — Qwen3-Coder-Next 80B MoE (~46GB, ~3B active, 262K+), SWE-bench ~70.6 — the frontier-adjacent brain none of the 24–32GB cards can touch. And it runs the 70B-dense class at Q4 plus the 30–35B MoEs at Q8 with a huge window. This is where "hunting intelligence" in local models actually pays.

Bottom line — "we're hunting intelligence"

  1. Intelligence scales with VRAM, not the card name. 3090 = 4090 in capability; the 5090 adds ~one step (fidelity + speed); the RTX 6000 Ada is the true IQ jump — the only card here that fits an 80B-MoE/70B-dense brain.
  2. Max local intelligence per dollar → RTX 6000 Ada running Qwen3-Coder-Next 80B (max IQ + MoE speed), with 70B-dense and dual-MoE as alternates. NVLink adds a future 96GB path.
  3. If speed + full-context + modern stack matter more than ceiling → 5090, but only with vLLM/TensorRT so the NVFP4 path is real; on Ollama it's just a fast 32GB card.
  4. 4090 over 3090 is almost never an intelligence upgrade. Spend the difference on capacity instead.
  5. Be honest about the frontier. GLM-5.2 / DeepSeek V4 are API-only at 10–30× your size and lead on hard reasoning + 1M context. At 0.70 $/hr even the 6000 Ada can cost more per token than DeepSeek V4-Flash — local is a privacy / sovereignty / unlimited-volume play now, and the only way to hold a frontier-adjacent brain entirely in your own hands.
  6. Model selection dominates hardware. Qwen3-Coder-Next 80B or Qwen3.8-27B (reasoning), Ornith-1.5-35B-A3B (agentic coding), DeepSeek-R1:32b (hard math). The card only decides how high a quant / how long a context you can afford it at — and on the 48GB card, whether you finally reach the 80B class.
